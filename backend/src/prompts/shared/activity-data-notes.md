How to read RECENT ACTIVITIES — one line per ride, columns in this order:
  date | duration | average HR | distance | minutes per HR zone | power | athlete rating
"hr=n/a" means no heart rate was recorded on that ride (strap forgotten or not paired): it is NOT an easy ride. Its zones are n/a too; judge its intensity from its power figures and rpe/feeling, and when it has neither, from duration alone and say so rather than guess.
Zone figures are minutes spent in each Garmin HR zone (z1=lowest, z5=highest intensity), from Garmin's default 5-zone system based on max HR — boundaries may differ slightly from the athlete's custom LTHR zones. "zones=n/a" means Garmin supplied no zone breakdown for that ride; "unrated" means the athlete answered neither post-ride prompt.
rpe = athlete-reported perceived exertion after the ride (1=very easy, 5=moderate, 10=maximal effort). Absent = not rated.
feeling = athlete-reported post-ride feeling (1=exhausted/very tired, 2=tired, 3=normal, 4=good, 5=strong/excellent). Absent = not rated.
When rpe and feeling are present, weight them heavily — they are direct athlete feedback on recovery state. High rpe (≥8) or low feeling (≤2) after a session signals real fatigue even if HR data looks moderate.
The reverse matters just as much: HR zones can be set wrong. When rides repeatedly show long Z4/Z5 time (30+ minutes) at rpe ≤ 7 with feeling ≥ 3, the zone boundaries are most likely too low for this athlete. Judge the intensity of those rides by rpe and feeling, do NOT read them as overreaching or as a reason to prescribe easier riding, and say in loadAssessment.insight that the HR profile may need re-checking. An HR CALIBRATION line under HR PROFILE, when present, states this from the numbers.
POWER — only rides from a bike with a power meter carry it; the athlete rides more than one bike. "power=n/a" means that bike has no power meter and says NOTHING about the effort: never mark a ride down, ask for power or read anything into its absence. Otherwise: avg = average watts, np = Normalized Power (weights the surges, so it is what the ride actually cost), if = np / FTP (intensity factor, only when an FTP is known), best20 = best 20-minute average watts, max = peak watts.
Power responds instantly; heart rate lags, drifts upward over long efforts and rises with heat, fatigue and caffeine. Where both exist, power says what was ridden and heart rate, rpe and feeling say what it cost. A ride where power held steady while HR drifted up was ridden as intended; HR at a given power that sits clearly higher than on the athlete's other rides is a fatigue signal worth weighing.
if and np cover the whole ride, warm-up and recoveries included, so an interval session reads well below its work-interval target. Rough whole-ride if: easy Z2 ride 0.55-0.75, Tempo session 0.75-0.85, Threshold or VO2Max session 0.80-0.95; Sprint sessions stay low because the sprints are short. Use these as a cross-check on HR, never as targets.
Prescriptions stay in heart rate zones. Workouts are synced to Garmin with HR targets because the athlete may ride a bike without a power meter, so never put watts in step labels.
